Signs of a Slab Leak in a Florida Home

A warm patch on the floor is the sign most people notice first.

The signs of a slab leak in a Boynton Beach home are easy to miss until the damage is obvious. Homes here sit on concrete slabs with no basement, and in mid century construction the copper supply lines were poured straight into that slab. When one fails, the water has nowhere to go but sideways.

Catching it early is the whole game. Found in the first weeks, a slab leak is usually a located spot repair. Left running for months, it washes out the fill under the slab and the bill moves from plumbing to flooring and drywall.

The early signs

  • A warm patch on a tile or wood floor, which means the hot line.
  • The sound of running water with every tap and appliance closed.
  • A water bill that climbed with no change in how you use water.
  • Hairline cracks that keep widening across a floor or wall.
  • Floor covering that lifts, cups or starts to sound hollow.
  • A musty smell that returns after cleaning.

One of these on its own can have other causes. Two together is worth a call.

Confirm it before you call

You can narrow this down yourself in two hours and it costs nothing. The water meter test tells you whether water is escaping somewhere you cannot see.

If the meter moves with everything shut, the next question is where. Shutting the valve on top of the water heater is a useful second check. If the sound stops, the leak is on the hot side, which fits the warm floor symptom. A bill that climbed first is common, and the other causes of a high bill are worth ruling out.

What the repair looks like

Slab leak repair routes

  • Spot repair. When it fits: One located leak, sound pipe elsewhere Trade-off: Smallest job, opens the floor at one point
  • Overhead reroute. When it fits: Repeat failures on one run Trade-off: Avoids the slab, some drywall work
  • Partial repipe. When it fits: One zone failing Trade-off: Middle ground on cost and disruption
  • Full repipe. When it fits: Third leak or widespread failure Trade-off: Largest job, ends the cycle

Locating comes first either way. Opening the floor to look is the most expensive way to find a leak, and it is avoidable. If water is pooling now, shut the main off before anything else.

Frequently asked questions

Can a slab leak fix itself?

No. Pressurised water does not stop escaping, and the opening gets larger rather than smaller. A leak that seems to come and go is usually one you only notice intermittently.

Will insurance cover it?

Policies commonly cover the resulting water damage while excluding the pipe repair. Keep the written diagnosis and call your carrier before the repair starts.

How much floor has to come up?

With proper locating, usually a small area at one point. That is the reason to pay for location work rather than exploratory digging.
