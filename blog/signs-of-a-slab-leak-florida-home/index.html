Signs of a Slab Leak in a Florida Home

A warm patch on the floor is the sign most people notice first.

The signs of a slab leak in a Boynton Beach home are easy to miss until the damage is obvious. Homes here sit on concrete slabs with no basement, and in mid century construction the copper supply lines were poured straight into that slab. When one fails, the water has nowhere to go but sideways.

Catching it early is the whole game. Found in the first weeks, a slab leak is usually a located spot repair. Left running for months, it washes out the fill under the slab and the bill moves from plumbing to flooring and drywall.

The early signs

  • A warm patch on a tile or wood floor, which means the hot line.
  • The sound of running water with every tap and appliance closed.
  • A water bill that climbed with no change in how you use water.
  • Hairline cracks that keep widening across a floor or wall.
  • Floor covering that lifts, cups or starts to sound hollow.
  • A musty smell that returns after cleaning.

One of these on its own can have other causes. Two together is worth a call.

Confirm it before you call

You can narrow this down yourself in two hours and it costs nothing. The water meter test tells you whether water is escaping somewhere you cannot see.

If the meter moves with everything shut, the next question is where. Shutting the valve on top of the water heater is a useful second check. If the sound stops, the leak is on the hot side, which fits the warm floor symptom. A bill that climbed first is common, and the other causes of a high bill are worth ruling out.

What the repair looks like

Slab leak repair routes

  • Spot repair. When it fits: One located leak, sound pipe elsewhere. Trade-off: Smallest job, opens the floor at one point.
  • Overhead reroute. When it fits: Repeat failures on one run. Trade-off: Avoids the slab, some drywall work.
  • Partial repipe. When it fits: One zone failing. Trade-off: Middle ground on cost and disruption.
  • Full repipe. When it fits: Third leak or widespread failure. Trade-off: Largest job, ends the cycle.

Locating comes first either way. Opening the floor to look is the most expensive way to find a leak, and it is avoidable. If water is pooling now, shut the main off before anything else.

Why slab leaks are common in this part of Florida

Most homes here are built on slab-on-grade foundations with the water lines run through or under the concrete. That construction is sound and well suited to the region, but it means the supply plumbing is embedded rather than accessible, and anything that attacks the pipe does so out of sight.

Three local conditions shorten the life of those lines. Copper in direct contact with concrete or soil can corrode from the outside, particularly where it was installed without a sleeve. The water chemistry in parts of the county is aggressive enough to pit copper from the inside. And high line pressure, which is common where a regulator has failed or was never fitted, accelerates both.

The result is a pipe that develops a pinhole rather than a burst. A pinhole under a slab can run for months before anything reaches the surface, which is exactly why the bill and the meter are better early indicators than anything you can see.

Ruling out the cheaper explanations first

What else produces the same symptoms

  • Warm spot on the floor. Slab leak: Hot line leaking below. Other common cause: Sun through a window, recessed light below, pet bed.
  • High water bill. Slab leak: Yes. Other common cause: Running toilet, irrigation leak, filled pool.
  • Sound of water with taps off. Slab leak: Yes. Other common cause: Refrigerator ice maker, pool pump, neighbor's line.
  • Damp baseboard or carpet edge. Slab leak: Yes. Other common cause: Air conditioning condensate line, exterior water intrusion.
  • Cracked tile or raised flooring. Slab leak: Possible. Other common cause: Normal settlement, failed thinset, moisture from outside.

The air conditioning condensate line deserves special mention here, because it produces a great deal of water, it runs for hours a day in this climate, and it is routinely mistaken for a plumbing leak. Finding where the water is coming from before anyone opens a floor saves more money than any other step in this process.

What to do and not do before the plumber arrives

  1. Run the meter test and write down the result. A confirmed leak changes the visit from an investigation into a location job.
  2. Isolate the irrigation at its valve and run the test again. This single step rules out the most commonly misdiagnosed cause.
  3. Photograph everything. Warm spots, damp areas, cracks, and the meter dial before and after. Dated photographs matter if an insurance claim follows.
  4. Keep the water on unless water is actively appearing in the house. A located leak is cheaper to fix than a guessed one, and the leak needs to be running to be found.
  5. Do not start removing flooring or baseboard. Electronic location works through finished floors, and demolition before location usually turns out to be in the wrong room.

If water is appearing at floor level inside the house, shut the main off and leave it off. At that point you are limiting damage, not preserving evidence.

Frequently asked questions

Can a slab leak fix itself?

No. Pressurised water does not stop escaping, and the opening gets larger rather than smaller. A leak that seems to come and go is usually one you only notice intermittently.

Will insurance cover it?

Policies commonly cover the resulting water damage while excluding the pipe repair. Keep the written diagnosis and call your carrier before the repair starts.

How much floor has to come up?

With proper locating, usually a small area at one point. That is the reason to pay for location work rather than exploratory digging.

What should I do right now?

How do plumbers find a slab leak without breaking the floor?

Acoustic listening equipment picks up the sound of water escaping under pressure through concrete, and a trained operator narrows it to a small area. Pressure isolation confirms which line is involved, hot or cold. Thermal imaging helps on hot lines. Tracer gas is used where sound alone is inconclusive. Opening the floor comes last, at the located point, and only once there is agreement on the repair method.

Can a slab leak damage the foundation?

Over a long period, yes. Continuously saturated soil under a slab can wash out fines and create voids, and that is what leads to settlement and cracking. This is why a confirmed slab leak is worth acting on promptly even when the water bill is manageable. The repair is almost always cheaper than the consequences of leaving it.

Is rerouting better than breaking the slab?

Often, and sometimes clearly so. Rerouting abandons the failed section and runs a new line overhead or through walls, which avoids concrete work entirely. It suits homes where the leak is in an awkward position, where the existing pipe is old enough that more failures are likely, or where finished floors are expensive to replace. A spot repair makes more sense on newer plumbing with a single identified cause.
