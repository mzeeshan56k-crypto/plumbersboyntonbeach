Hydro Jetting vs Snaking: Which Does Your Line Need?

One punches a hole through the blockage. The other restores the full pipe diameter.

Choosing between hydro jetting and snaking comes down to what is in the pipe. Snaking, also called cabling, pushes a steel cable through the blockage and breaks it up. Hydro jetting sends water through a nozzle at high pressure and scours the pipe wall clean.

Both have a place. Cabling is faster and cheaper on a single soft clog. Jetting costs more and does more, because it restores the full diameter instead of opening a channel through the middle of the build-up.

What snaking does well

A cable is the right tool when something is stuck and the pipe is otherwise sound. Hair in a shower branch. A toy in a toilet. A soft blockage in a kitchen line that has not been building for years.

It is quick, it is inexpensive and in many homes it is all the line needs. The limit is what it leaves behind. A cable opens a path through grease and scale. It does not remove the layer that caused the clog.

What jetting does well

Jetting is the answer when the pipe wall itself is coated. Grease in a long kitchen run. Scale in older cast iron. Fine roots that have worked through a joint.

Because it cleans back to the pipe wall, the line stays clear far longer. That is why we use it on main lines and on any drain that has been cabled more than once. If roots are the cause, roots in a Palm Beach County sewer line explains what happens next.

How to choose

Picking the right method

  • One slow fixture, first time. Better choice: Cabling Why: Fast, cheap and usually enough
  • Kitchen line that re-clogs. Better choice: Jetting Why: Removes the grease layer, not just a channel
  • Main line backing up. Better choice: Jetting with a camera Why: Diameter matters and you need to see the cause
  • Known cracked or collapsed pipe. Better choice: Neither on its own Why: Repair first. Cleaning will not hold

We camera the line before jetting on anything older. A cracked pipe needs repair rather than pressure. A camera inspection settles that question before money is spent.

What it costs you to guess

Repeat cabling on a line that needs jetting is the most common waste we see. Three visits in two years costs more than one proper clean, and the line is worse at the end of it.

Going the other way is rarer but it happens. Jetting a simple hair clog is money spent on capability you did not need. The camera is what keeps both mistakes off your invoice.

Frequently asked questions

Does hydro jetting damage pipes?

Not when the pipe is sound and the pressure is matched to it. The risk is jetting a line that is already cracked or badly corroded, which is why the camera comes first.

How long does jetting last?

On a kitchen line with normal use, several years. The point of jetting is that it removes the build-up, so there is nothing left for the next clog to form on.

Can I rent a drain snake and do it myself?

For a slow bathroom sink, often yes. For anything past the trap, a rented cable is easy to snap or jam in the line, and retrieving it costs more than the original job.
