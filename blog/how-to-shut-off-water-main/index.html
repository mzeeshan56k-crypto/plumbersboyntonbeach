How to Shut Off the Water in a Boynton Beach Home

Find it today. You do not want to be looking for it during a burst.

Knowing how to shut off the water in your Boynton Beach home is the single most useful thing a homeowner can learn. When a supply line lets go, the difference between a wet floor and a ruined room is how quickly the main valve closes.

Most homes here have two options. A valve on the house side, usually near a hose bib or in the garage, and the meter valve at the street. One of them will work. The time to find out which is now, not during the emergency.

Where to look

  • Near the front hose bib, on the wall or just inside it.
  • In the garage, close to where the supply enters.
  • In the meter box at the street, under the lid.
  • On a water heater, the cold inlet valve shuts that appliance only.

Florida homes rarely have basements, so the valve is usually at ground level and outdoors. If you cannot find it, the meter box always works.

Test it before you need it

Old valves seize. A shutoff that has not moved in ten years may not close when it matters, and discovering that during a burst is the worst possible timing. It is the same check worth doing when a slab leak is suspected.

Turn it fully closed and open again twice a year. If it will not move or it weeps after closing, have it replaced. That is a small job now and a flooded room later.

What to do when a pipe lets go

  1. Close the main valve. Clockwise to shut.
  2. Open a low tap to drain the remaining pressure in the lines.
  3. Kill power at the breaker to any circuit near standing water.
  4. Move what you can and photograph the damage.
  5. Call a plumber and say a line has burst.

Do not walk into standing water that may be touching electrics. Shut the breaker first or wait for help.

Frequently asked questions

Which way closes the valve?

Clockwise for almost all of them. A lever style quarter turn valve closes when the handle sits across the pipe rather than along it.

The valve will not budge. Now what?

Do not force it. Use the meter valve at the street instead and have the house valve replaced afterwards.

Should I shut off water when I travel?

Yes, if the house will be empty for more than a few days. It is the cheapest insurance against an unattended leak, and it avoids the kind of bill covered in why a water bill doubles. Run the meter test before you leave.
