The Water Meter Test That Confirms a Hidden Leak

Two hours, no tools and no cost. It is the test worth running first.

The water meter test is the cheapest way to confirm a hidden leak in a Boynton Beach home. It takes two hours, needs no tools and tells you something a plumber would otherwise charge to establish. If the meter moves while nothing is running, water is escaping somewhere you cannot see.

It will not tell you where the leak is. It will tell you whether you have one, and that is the question worth answering before you book anything.

How to run it

  1. Turn off every tap, appliance, ice maker and water filter in the house.
  2. Find the water meter, usually in a box near the street.
  3. Write down the full reading, including the small sweep dial.
  4. Use no water at all for two hours. No flushing.
  5. Read it again. Any movement means water is leaving the system.

Check that nobody else is home before you start. One flush invalidates the whole test.

Narrowing it down

If the meter moved, repeat the test with the irrigation system isolated at its valve. If the movement stops, the leak is in the irrigation rather than the house. Irrigation is one of the usual causes of a high bill, and the repair is completely different from a leak inside the house.

If it still moves, the leak is inside the building envelope. A warm patch on the floor points to the slab, which the slab leak signs cover in more detail.

What the numbers mean

Reading the result

  • No movement in two hours. What it usually means: No active supply leak. Look at drains or appliances instead.
  • Slow movement. What it usually means: A small leak, often a toilet flapper or an irrigation valve.
  • Steady visible movement. What it usually means: A significant leak. Shut the main and call.
  • Movement only when irrigation is open. What it usually means: The leak is in the yard lines.

A running toilet is the most common cause of slow movement and the cheapest to fix. A meter test will not catch a waste-side problem at all, though, since nothing on the drain side is pressurized. A camera inspection is what finds those.

Reading your meter correctly

Most meters in this area have a large dial with a sweep hand and a smaller indicator, often a triangle or a small spinning disc, called the leak detector. That small indicator is the sensitive one. It will turn on flows far too small to move the main sweep hand, which is exactly what a pinhole or a slow toilet flapper produces.

Digital meters work differently. Many display a flow rate alongside the total, and some show a leak indicator symbol. If yours only shows the cumulative total, the test still works, it simply needs patience. Write the full reading down including every digit, wait, and compare.

The lid is often tight and usually full of sand and insects. A flathead screwdriver in the slot lifts it. Look before reaching in, and expect ants. If the box itself is full of water with the house water off, that is its own finding and it means the leak is on the service line before it reaches the house.

Narrowing it down room by room

  1. Confirm the leak with the whole house closed. Everything off, including ice makers and any filtration system with an automatic flush cycle.
  2. Close the irrigation isolation valve and retest. Irrigation is the single most common source of a mystery bill in this county.
  3. Close the valve at the pool equipment if there is an autofill, and retest.
  4. Shut the water heater's cold inlet and retest. Movement now points at the cold side, no movement points at the hot side or the heater.
  5. Close the angle stop behind each toilet one at a time, retesting between. Toilets are the most frequent interior culprit by a wide margin.
  6. Close the main valve at the house. If the meter still moves with the house isolated, the leak is in the service line between the meter and the building.

That last step is the most valuable one in the sequence, because it separates a repair inside your walls from a repair in your yard. The two have completely different costs and completely different urgency.

What the test will not catch

  • A drain leak. Nothing on the waste side is pressurized, so no water passes the meter. A drain leak shows as damp or odor, not as consumption.
  • An intermittent leak. A flapper that only seeps after a flush, or a valve that weeps when pressure peaks at night, can pass a short test cleanly.
  • A leak downstream of a closed valve. If you have already shut something off to stop a problem, the test will not see it.
  • A leak on a well system, which has no meter at all. There, a pump that cycles with no water being used is the equivalent signal.
  • Very small losses over a short window. If the bill suggests a leak and a thirty minute test is clean, run it overnight before concluding anything.

When the test is clean but the bill is not, the next step is usually pressure-based rather than volume-based. Proper leak detection starts by isolating sections and watching pressure hold or fall, which catches what a meter test misses.

Frequently asked questions

How long should the test run?

Two hours is enough to show anything that matters. Overnight is better if you can manage it. If the meter is racing rather than creeping, shut the main off and call.

My meter has no sweep dial. What then?

Record the full digital reading including the smallest digits. Photograph it so there is no argument about what it said.

Does a pool count?

Yes, if it has an autofill. Isolate it before testing or the result will be misleading.

What if the test is positive?

Call our Boynton Beach plumbers on (833) 567-2788 and say the meter is moving. That tells us to bring locating equipment. Leak detection explains what happens next.

How long should I leave the test running?

Thirty minutes catches most active leaks. For a suspected slow leak, or when a short test comes back clean against a high bill, read the meter at night before bed and again first thing in the morning with nobody using water. An eight hour window makes very small losses obvious.

The meter box is full of water. What does that mean?

With the house water off and the meter still turning, water in the box points at a leak on the service line between the meter and the house. That section is normally the homeowner's responsibility from the meter onward, with the utility responsible on its side. Either way it is worth reporting to the utility and having located before anyone digs.

Can I use the test to check a repair worked?

Yes, and it is the best confirmation available. Run the same test the same way after the work is finished. A still indicator on a test that previously moved is objective proof, and it is worth doing before the plumber leaves rather than a week later.
