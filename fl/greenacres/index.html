Condos and 55-plus

Shared Stacks and Community Rules

Greenacres has a large share of condo and 55-plus communities, and plumbing in those buildings behaves differently. When several units share a drain stack, a blockage downstream shows up as a backup in the lowest unit, even though the cause is two floors above.

We diagnose stack problems properly rather than repeatedly cabling a fixture that is not at fault, and we work with association management on access, scheduling and who owns which section of pipe. Preventive service

A Plumbing Inspection Checklist for Greenacres Homes

In a neighborhood where everything was installed the same year, an hour of inspection prevents most of the emergency calls we take.

Angle stops and supply lines. Under every sink and behind every toilet. Multi-turn stops from the 1970s and 1980s seize, and old risers crack at the ferrule without warning. Quarter-turn valves and braided stainless lines are inexpensive to fit.

Water heater age and pan. Read the serial number for the build year, check for rust at the base, and confirm a drain pan is present and piped outside. Past ten years on local water, plan the replacement rather than waiting for the flood.

Shutoff location and function. Find the main valve now and make sure it actually turns. A seized main shutoff turns a small leak into a whole-house problem.

Drain flow and cast iron. If original cast iron branches are still in service, a camera inspection tells you whether you have years left or months.
