Water quality

Well Water, Iron and Sulfur in Wellington

Plenty of Wellington properties, particularly the larger acreage lots and equestrian parcels, run on private wells. That water arrives untreated, and out here it commonly carries dissolved iron, hydrogen sulfide and high hardness.

The signs are unmistakable: orange staining in toilets and sinks, a rotten egg smell that is strongest on hot water, and laundry that comes out looking worse than it went in. Softening alone does not fix this. Iron has to be oxidized and filtered out before the softener, or the resin bed fouls within a year.

We test the water on site, then build the treatment stack in the right order for your specific results rather than selling a packaged system that ignores them.

Irrigation and backflow

Big Lots Mean Big Irrigation Systems

Wellington lots are large and heavily irrigated, and any irrigation system tied to the potable supply needs a backflow preventer that is tested annually. Village of Wellington Utilities enforces this, and missed tests bring notices and fees.

The plumbers we dispatch hold the certification required to test, rebuild and replace these assemblies, and the report is filed with the utility. Sandy soil and constant sun are hard on them here, so devices that passed comfortably two years ago often fail on relief valve pressure now.

Equestrian properties add barn hydrants, wash racks and automatic waterers to the mix. Those lines freeze up with mineral buildup and take real abuse, and they are worth inspecting before show season rather than during it.

Good to know

Wellington Plumbing: Village Utilities, Wells and Equestrian Properties

Wellington sits west of the coastal ridge, and almost everything about its plumbing follows from that.

Village of Wellington utilities

The village operates its own water and wastewater utility, drawing from local wellfields, and it administers its own cross-connection control program. Irrigation systems connected to potable supply require annual certified backflow testing filed with the village. Permits for water heaters, repipes and gas work also go through Wellington rather than the county.

Private wells on larger parcels

Many acreage lots and equestrian parcels run on private wells with untreated water carrying dissolved iron, hydrogen sulfide and high hardness. Treatment has to be sequenced correctly, with oxidation and iron filtration ahead of any softener, or the resin bed fouls within a year. Pressure tanks, switches and pumps need checking too, since short cycling often gets blamed on treatment equipment.

Barns, wash racks and irrigation

Equestrian properties add barn hydrants, wash racks, automatic waterers and large irrigation systems to the plumbing picture. Those lines take hard use and build mineral deposits quickly, and they sit exposed to sun and weather year round. Inspecting them ahead of show season is far easier than repairing them during it.

Five minutes, twice a year

What Should Wellington Homeowners Check Themselves?

No equipment needed, and each check targets something that quietly gets worse until it does not.

  • Have the well water tested if treatment has never been sized against a result.
  • Check the irrigation backflow device and its certification date.
  • Shut the irrigation valve and re-read the meter to separate yard leaks from house leaks.
  • Check the water heater pan and drain line, especially in attic or closet installs.
  • Walk outbuildings and barns; their lines are the ones people forget.

What Plumbing Work Do We Do in Wellington?

Every service below is available at Wellington addresses, on the same schedule and the same flat-rate pricing we use in Boynton Beach.

Emergency Plumber

When a supply line lets go or sewage backs up through a floor drain, the first job is stopping the water and the second is telling you what the repair costs. We walk you through the shutoff on the phone, arrive with a stocked truck, and hand you a written flat price before anything is opened up. Wellington's homes lean newer, so we see fitting and manifold failures on PEX far more often than pipe collapse.

Drain Cleaning

A drain that clears and re-clogs within weeks is telling you the pipe itself is the problem. We cable or hydro jet the line to get flow back, then run a camera so you can see whether you are dealing with a clog, a belly or a break. Irrigation and well lines account for a surprising share of Wellington's hidden leaks, not just interior plumbing.

Water Heater Repair & Installation

No hot water, rusty hot water or a puddle under the tank each point somewhere different. We test elements, thermostats, gas valves and the anode rod before quoting, because a failed part costs a fraction of a new heater. When the tank really is finished, we replace 40 to 80 gallon units with the permit and haul-away included. Larger floor plans here often justify a recirculation loop or a second unit rather than one oversized heater.

Tankless Water Heater Installation

Going tankless works when the gas line, venting and electrical capacity are right for the unit. We measure your actual fixture demand first, then install with isolation valves so annual descaling is a short service call rather than a teardown. Wellington runs its own utilities, so we confirm capacity and permitting before committing to a unit.

Leak Detection and Repair

A water bill that doubled with nothing visible means water is escaping somewhere you cannot see. Acoustic listening equipment, thermal imaging and pressure isolation let us pinpoint hidden leaks in walls, slabs, irrigation and pool lines, usually within inches. Demolition is the last resort here, never the opening move. Wellington's larger floor plans mean long runs, so isolating a failure takes more diagnostic work than a small lot.

Slab Leak Repair

A slab leak shows up as a warm spot underfoot, a meter that turns with everything off, or a crack that keeps widening. We pinpoint it without opening the floor first, then quote the three honest options: spot repair, overhead reroute, or repipe. Irrigation and well lines account for a real share of hidden leaks here, not just interior plumbing.

Sewer Line Repair and Replacement

Repeat main line backups, sewage smell in the yard and a soggy strip of lawn mean the lateral itself has failed. We run a recorded camera inspection the full length of the pipe, locate every defect and its depth, then quote spot repair, trenchless lining or full replacement based on what the footage actually shows. Homes in 33449 are newer still, and their failures are almost entirely at fittings.

Pipe Repair and Installation

We repair single failures and repipe whole houses in copper, CPVC or PEX. Runs go through the attic and walls with the smallest openings that will do the job, and every line is pressure tested and inspected before the drywall is closed back up. Equestrian properties carry outbuildings and yard lines that are easy to forget when a meter keeps turning.

Toilet Repair and Installation

Most toilet complaints are a flapper, a fill valve or a wax ring, and all three are same-visit fixes. The ones worth taking seriously are water at the base and a bowl that rocks, because both mean the flange seal is leaking into your subfloor. We repair, reseal or install new comfort-height units. Equestrian properties and large family homes mean high fixture counts, and we plan the work around that.

Faucet & Fixture Installation

We set faucets, shower valves, tub spouts and bidet seats, and we replace the angle stops and supply lines while we are in there. A seized shutoff is what turns a simple future repair into a house-wide water shutdown, so it gets handled now. Large homes with high fixture counts need pressure and flow checked, not just the fixture swapped.

Kitchen and Bathroom Plumbing

Remodel plumbing runs in two stages: rough-in while the walls are open, then trim once the tile and cabinets are set. Getting drain slope, valve depth and vent placement right at rough-in is what prevents the classic remodel problems. We coordinate both visits with your contractor and manage the inspections. High fixture counts here mean pressure and flow get checked, not just the fixture swapped.

Garbage Disposal Repair & Installation

Humming means jammed, silence means power, and a drip from the bottom seam means the motor seal is gone. We check the trap arm pitch and the dishwasher high loop at the same time, because the drain is often the real reason a disposal keeps backing up. Large family homes in 33414 often need several fixtures handled in one visit to be worth the trip.

Gas Line Repair and Installation

New gas runs for ranges, dryers, pool heaters, generators and outdoor kitchens are sized to the appliance load, pressure tested and permitted. Buried steel in damp, sandy South Florida soil degrades faster than it does inland, so we inspect existing runs before tying anything new into them. Well water, irrigation systems and fertigation lines make filtration and backflow testing routine in 33414 and 33449.

Backflow Testing and Prevention

If you have received a testing notice, the deadline is real and the report has to reach the utility. We test the assembly, repair or replace it if it fails, and submit the certification on your behalf so the file is closed. The Village of Wellington permits this work, and annual backflow reports are filed on your behalf.

Water Filtration Installation

South Florida water is hard enough to scale a water heater years early and leave spotting on every fixture. We size softeners, carbon tanks and reverse osmosis systems to your actual water test and household demand rather than a shelf package, and set them where service access stays easy. The Village of Wellington runs its own water and wastewater utility and administers its own cross-connection program.

Sump Pump Installation and Repair

Sump pumps fail quietly, and you find out during the storm. We test the float, discharge and check valve, replace worn units, and fit battery backup so the pump still runs when the power does not. Well supply out here makes filtration a sizing exercise against a water test rather than a package choice.

FAQs

What Do Wellington Homeowners Ask Us Most?

Do you service private well systems?

Yes, including pressure tanks, pumps, iron filtration, softening and sulfur removal. We test the water before recommending equipment.

Why does my water smell like rotten eggs?

Hydrogen sulfide, common in well water out west. It is treatable with oxidation and filtration, and it is worse on the hot side.

Do you do backflow testing in Wellington?

Yes, certified testing with the report filed directly with Village of Wellington Utilities.

Can you plumb barns and outbuildings?

Yes. Barn hydrants, wash racks, automatic waterers and dedicated supply runs are all work we do here.

Do you handle well water systems and irrigation lines?

Yes. Wellington has a lot of properties on well supply with substantial irrigation, and both are regular work. Treatment is sized against an actual water test rather than a package, and irrigation backflow is tested and certified to the village's requirements.
