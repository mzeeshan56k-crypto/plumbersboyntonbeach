Water quality

Well Water, Iron and Sulfur in Wellington

Plenty of Wellington properties, particularly the larger acreage lots and equestrian parcels, run on private wells. That water arrives untreated, and out here it commonly carries dissolved iron, hydrogen sulfide and high hardness.

The signs are unmistakable: orange staining in toilets and sinks, a rotten egg smell that is strongest on hot water, and laundry that comes out looking worse than it went in. Softening alone does not fix this. Iron has to be oxidized and filtered out before the softener, or the resin bed fouls within a year.

We test the water on site, then build the treatment stack in the right order for your specific results rather than selling a packaged system that ignores them.

Irrigation and backflow

Big Lots Mean Big Irrigation Systems

Wellington lots are large and heavily irrigated, and any irrigation system tied to the potable supply needs a backflow preventer that is tested annually. Village of Wellington Utilities enforces this, and missed tests bring notices and fees.

We are certified to test, rebuild and replace these assemblies, and we file the report with the utility. Sandy soil and constant sun are hard on them here, so devices that passed comfortably two years ago often fail on relief valve pressure now.

Equestrian properties add barn hydrants, wash racks and automatic waterers to the mix. Those lines freeze up with mineral buildup and take real abuse, and they are worth inspecting before show season rather than during it.

Good to know

Wellington Plumbing: Village Utilities, Wells and Equestrian Properties

Wellington sits west of the coastal ridge, and almost everything about its plumbing follows from that.

Village of Wellington utilities

The village operates its own water and wastewater utility, drawing from local wellfields, and it administers its own cross-connection control program. Irrigation systems connected to potable supply require annual certified backflow testing filed with the village. Permits for water heaters, repipes and gas work also go through Wellington rather than the county.

Private wells on larger parcels

Many acreage lots and equestrian parcels run on private wells with untreated water carrying dissolved iron, hydrogen sulfide and high hardness. Treatment has to be sequenced correctly, with oxidation and iron filtration ahead of any softener, or the resin bed fouls within a year. Pressure tanks, switches and pumps need checking too, since short cycling often gets blamed on treatment equipment.

Barns, wash racks and irrigation

Equestrian properties add barn hydrants, wash racks, automatic waterers and large irrigation systems to the plumbing picture. Those lines take hard use and build mineral deposits quickly, and they sit exposed to sun and weather year round. Inspecting them ahead of show season is far easier than repairing them during it.

FAQs

Wellington Plumbing Questions, Answered

Do you service private well systems?

Yes, including pressure tanks, pumps, iron filtration, softening and sulfur removal. We test the water before recommending equipment.

Why does my water smell like rotten eggs?

Hydrogen sulfide, common in well water out west. It is treatable with oxidation and filtration, and it is worse on the hot side.

Do you do backflow testing in Wellington?

Yes, certified testing with the report filed directly with Village of Wellington Utilities.

Can you plumb barns and outbuildings?

Yes. Barn hydrants, wash racks, automatic waterers and dedicated supply runs are all work we do here.
