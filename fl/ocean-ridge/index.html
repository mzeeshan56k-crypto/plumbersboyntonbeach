Ocean Ridge is a small town of mostly single-family homes between the ocean and the Intracoastal, many built decades ago and renovated several times since. Knowing what is behind the walls before opening them matters here.

Island conditions

Salt, Humidity and the Water Table

Three forces work against plumbing on the island. Salt air corrodes anything exposed. Constant humidity keeps utility spaces damp, which rusts water heater bases and supports. And the water table sits close to the surface, so anything buried is effectively in wet soil year round.

That last point matters most for sewer laterals and irrigation lines. Buried metal degrades fast, and a leak in saturated sand shows no surface sign at all. We pressure test rather than guess.

Inside the house, the practical advice is the same as everywhere on the coast: solid brass fixtures, stainless hardware, a drain pan under every water heater, and an annual look at anything exposed to outside air.

Renovated homes

Mixed Pipe in Renovated Beach Houses

Most Ocean Ridge homes have been renovated at least once, sometimes three or four times. Each renovation added a new section of pipe in whatever material was standard at the time, so it is common to find galvanized, copper, CPVC and PEX all in the same house.

Mixed systems create their own problems, particularly where dissimilar metals meet without a dielectric union and corrode at the joint. Before quoting any significant work we map what is actually installed, so the repair addresses the real system rather than an assumed one.

Seasonal homes

Protecting an Ocean Ridge Home While You Are Away

Many homes here sit empty for part of the year, and an unattended leak is the single most expensive thing that can happen to them.

Shut the water off at the main. A failed supply line in an empty house can run for weeks. Closing one valve removes that risk entirely, and it costs nothing.

Consider an automatic shutoff valve. Leak detection valves monitor flow and close the water on their own when they see a pattern that looks like a leak. For a seasonal property, this is the highest-value plumbing upgrade available.

Water heater on vacation mode. Turn it down or off rather than heating an empty house for months, which also slows scale buildup inside the tank.

Before you leave and when you return. Have exposed fittings, the heater pan, angle stops and irrigation checked once a year, ideally ahead of hurricane season, and flush the system when you come back.
