High-rise plumbing

Shared Stacks and Riser Problems

In a tower, your drain does not end at your wall. It joins a vertical stack shared with every unit above and below. When that stack narrows with grease and scale, the backup usually shows up in a lower-floor unit that did nothing wrong.

Diagnosing this properly saves owners real money. We determine whether the blockage is in your branch line or the building stack, and if it is the stack, we document it so the association handles the repair rather than the unit owner.

Water pressure complaints are the other common high-rise call. In tall buildings, pressure at upper floors depends on booster pumps and pressure-reducing valves that need service. A failing PRV in a unit shows up as either weak flow or unexpectedly high pressure that damages fixtures.

Coastal fixtures

Oceanfront Wear on Fixtures and Heaters

Balcony exposure and salt-heavy air work on everything in an oceanfront unit. Fixture finishes pit, angle stops seize, and water heaters in closets with poor ventilation corrode from the outside in.

For units used seasonally, the single best habit is shutting off the water supply when you leave. A failed supply line in an empty unit can run for weeks. We install quarter-turn main shutoffs and, where owners want it, automatic leak detection valves that close the water on their own.

Seasonal residents

Leak Prevention for Part-Time Highland Beach Condo Owners

In a tower, a leak in an empty unit becomes several neighbors’ problem, and associations take it seriously for good reason.

Shut off the unit supply when you leave. The single most effective step, and it takes ten seconds. If your unit shutoff is a stiff old multi-turn valve, have it replaced with a quarter-turn ball valve so it actually gets used.

Automatic leak detection. A monitoring valve that closes the water when it detects abnormal flow is inexpensive insurance against a claim that could involve multiple units, and some associations now encourage or require them.

Replace supply lines and angle stops. Braided stainless lines and modern stops behind toilets, under sinks and at the washer are the cheapest preventive work available in a condo.

Water heater in a closet. Confirm there is a drain pan piped to a drain. In a stacked building, a failed tank without a pan reaches the ceiling below quickly.

FAQs

What Do Highland Beach Homeowners Ask Us Most?

Do you work in Highland Beach condo towers?

Yes, routinely. We coordinate with management on access, shutoffs and the insurance paperwork buildings require.

Who pays when a shared stack backs up?

Usually the association, if the blockage is in the building stack rather than your branch line. We document which it is.

I only live here part of the year. What should I do before leaving?

Shut off the unit water supply, or install an automatic leak detection shutoff valve. It is the cheapest insurance there is.

Why is my water pressure low on a high floor?

Often a failing pressure-reducing valve in the unit or a building booster pump issue. We test the unit side first.

Who pays when a shared stack backs up in a condo?

That depends on your association's documents and where the failure sits. A blockage in the shared vertical stack is usually a building matter, while the branch inside your unit is usually yours. The camera inspection that locates it is also what settles the question, so get the diagnosis before the argument.
