Warning signs

Signs Your Home Needs Repiping Instead of Another Pipe Repair

These are the signals that the system, not just one length of pipe, has reached the end.

  • More than one pinhole leak in the past two years
  • Rusty or discolored water at multiple fixtures
  • Water pressure that keeps dropping year after year
  • Visible corrosion or green staining on copper
  • Galvanized steel pipe still in service
  • Polybutylene grey pipe anywhere in the home
  • Repeated slab leaks in the same house
  • Pipe noise, banging or whistling, that keeps returning

Polybutylene is a known failure risk and many insurers will not write a policy on a home that still has it. Replacement is usually not optional.

Material choice

PEX, CPVC or Copper Repiping: Choosing Pipe for a Florida Home

All three materials are code-approved for residential water lines. They behave differently in our climate and water, and that is what should drive the choice.

PEX-A

Flexible tubing run in long lengths with far fewer fittings inside walls, which removes the most common failure points. It resists scale, tolerates slab and attic conditions well, installs faster and costs less in both material and labor. For most Boynton Beach homes it is the sensible pick.

CPVC

Rigid plastic, widely used across Florida since the mid-1980s and inexpensive. Its weakness is long-term exposure to high attic heat, which makes it brittle over time. When we use it, attic runs get insulated and supported properly.

Copper

Long proven, rigid, and preferred by some homeowners for exposed runs and resale perception. It costs more in material and labor, and it remains vulnerable to the same water chemistry that caused the original failure, which is worth weighing carefully on a slab home.

What we recommend and why

PEX for concealed distribution, copper where pipe is exposed and appearance matters, and insulation on everything that crosses an attic. We quote more than one option so you compare real numbers rather than opinions.

Boynton Beach conditions

What Is in the Walls of Boynton Beach Homes

Housing here comes in layers. Homes from the 1950s and 1960s were plumbed in galvanized steel or copper. The 1970s and early 1980s brought polybutylene into many developments, and that pipe is now well past its safe life. From the mid-1980s onward CPVC took over, and PEX became standard in the last two decades.

Each has a different failure mode. Galvanized rusts closed and chokes pressure. Polybutylene fails at fittings without warning. Copper in a slab pits. CPVC gets brittle in hot attics after long exposure.

Attic heat is a genuinely local variable. A Boynton Beach attic runs well over 120 degrees in August, which accelerates aging in plastic pipe that was never insulated. When we repipe, lines running through attic space get insulated and supported properly, which is a detail plenty of older installs skipped.

What to expect

Living in Your Home During a Repipe: Timeline and Disruption

The word repipe sounds worse than the reality. Handled properly it is a two to four day job and almost nobody needs to move out.

Day one: planning and protection

Routes are mapped through attic and wall cavities to keep openings small and runs short. Floors get covered, furniture is moved clear of work walls, and dust barriers go up before any drywall is cut.

Days one to three: installation

New lines are run to every fixture, valves and manifolds are set, and each connection is made. Water is typically shut off during working hours and restored at the end of each day, so you have service overnight.

Pressure test and inspection

The system is held under pressure and checked, then the inspector signs off before any wall is closed. This is the step that cannot be rushed, and it is why permits matter.

Patch and finish

Drywall openings are patched and textured to match. Paint is usually left to you so the color matches your existing walls exactly, and we say so in the quote rather than surprising you at the end.

What changes afterward

Pressure returns to full at every fixture, discolored first-draw water disappears, and the risk of the next slab leak is gone. It is also a documented improvement at resale in a market where buyers ask about pipe age.

Before you call

What to Check Before a Repipe Conversation

Knowing a few things about your own system changes the quality of the advice you get.

  • Count the leaks you have had in the last two years and where they were.
  • Look under a sink and at the water heater to identify the pipe material.
  • Check your water pressure at a hose bib if you have a gauge; high pressure shortens pipe life.
  • Note whether hot lines fail more often than cold, which is typical of slab copper.

One thing not to do: If you find grey plastic supply pipe with crimped fittings, stop and get it looked at. Polybutylene fails at the fittings with little warning.

Urgency

When Does a Pipe Failure Need Same-Day Attention?

A weeping joint can wait a day or two. Call immediately if:

  • A line has burst and you cannot isolate it at a shutoff.
  • Water is running inside a wall or above a ceiling.
  • Pressure has dropped suddenly across the whole house.
  • You are getting repeat pinholes weeks apart, which means the system is failing, not the fitting.

If any of those describe what you are seeing, call (833) 567-2788 now rather than booking online. Emergency calls are moved to the front of the queue.

FAQs

Repiping FAQs

How long does a whole-home repipe take?

Two to four days for most single-family homes. Water is usually back on each evening.

Is PEX as good as copper?

For residential water lines, yes, and it handles hard water and slab conditions better. Copper still has a place in exposed runs.

Do I have to move out during a repipe?

Almost never. We work room by room and restore water service at the end of each day.

Will you patch the drywall?

Yes, patching and texture are part of the quote. Paint is usually left to you so the color matches.

Do repipes need a permit?

Yes, in Boynton Beach and Palm Beach County. We pull it, schedule the inspection and close it out.

Can I stay in the house during a repipe?

Usually yes. Work is staged so water is restored at the end of each day, and most of the disruption is drywall openings rather than lost service. You will be without water for parts of the day while sections are tied in, and that schedule is agreed before work starts.
