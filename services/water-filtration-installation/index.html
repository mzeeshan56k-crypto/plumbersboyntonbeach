Water filtration in Boynton Beach is less about taste than most people expect. It is about hardness. Palm Beach County water is treated and safe to drink, but it carries a heavy mineral load that shortens the life of water heaters, stains fixtures and leaves skin and laundry feeling rough. We run water filtration installation from how our Boynton Beach service works and across the county, including Hypoluxo and Lake Worth Beach.

The right system depends on what you actually want to solve. A softener handles scale. A carbon tank handles chlorine taste and odor. Reverse osmosis handles drinking water quality at one tap. Many homes benefit from a combination.

We test your water first, then recommend equipment sized to your household. There is no reason to buy a system rated for eight people when three live in the house.

Warning signs

Signs Your Home Needs a Water Softener or Filtration System

Hard water leaves the same evidence in every house. Look for these.

  • White crust on faucets, shower heads and glass doors
  • Soap that will not lather well
  • Stiff, dull laundry even with extra detergent
  • Water heaters failing years earlier than expected
  • Spots on dishes straight out of the dishwasher
  • Dry, itchy skin after showering
  • A chlorine taste or smell from the tap
  • Reduced flow from shower heads over time

Scale is doing the same thing inside your pipes and water heater that it does on your shower door. You just cannot see that part.

System types

Water Softener vs Carbon Filter vs Reverse Osmosis: What Solves What

These three systems solve different problems, and buying the wrong one is the most common mistake homeowners make here.

Water softener for hardness

Ion exchange removes calcium and magnesium, which is what causes scale on fixtures, spotting on glass, early water heater failure and stiff laundry. Sized by grain capacity against your hardness and household demand, with metered regeneration so it only regenerates when needed.

Catalytic carbon for taste and odor

A whole-house carbon tank removes chlorine taste and smell at the point of entry, with no salt and no drain requirement. Local relevance: much of Palm Beach County disinfects with chloramine, which ordinary carbon handles poorly. Catalytic carbon with adequate contact time is what actually works, and generic whole-house filters often underperform for exactly this reason.

Reverse osmosis for drinking water

RO removes dissolved solids that softeners and carbon do not touch. Usually installed under a kitchen sink with a dedicated faucet and an optional line to the refrigerator.

Salt-free conditioners

These change how scale forms rather than removing minerals. They add no sodium and need no drain, which suits some condos, but they do not soften water in the strict sense. We are clear about that difference.

Boynton Beach conditions

What Is Actually in Boynton Beach Water

Boynton Beach draws from the surficial aquifer and treats it at the East and West water treatment plants. The result is water that meets every safety standard and still tests hard, commonly in the 8 to 12 grains per gallon range. Anything above 7 is considered hard by industry standards.

Chloramine is used for disinfection in much of Palm Beach County. That matters because standard carbon filters remove free chlorine easily but need catalytic carbon and longer contact time to handle chloramine. Systems sold as generic whole-house filters often underperform here for exactly that reason.

Homes on private wells west of Boynton Beach face a different set of issues: iron staining, sulfur smell and variable hardness. Those need a different equipment stack, usually starting with oxidation and iron filtration before any softener.

Well water

Treating Well Water West of Boynton Beach: Iron, Sulfur and Hardness

Properties on private wells face a different problem set entirely, and equipment order matters more than equipment brand.

What the water usually contains

Dissolved iron, hydrogen sulfide and high hardness are the common trio. The evidence is orange staining in fixtures, a rotten egg smell strongest on the hot side, and laundry that comes out looking worse than it went in.

Why a softener alone fails

Iron fouls softener resin. Put a softener first in line on iron-bearing water and the resin bed is ruined within a year. Iron has to be oxidized and filtered out before the softener sees the water.

The correct sequence

Sediment filtration, then oxidation and iron or sulfur removal, then softening, then any point-of-use RO. Getting that order right is the whole job.

Pressure tanks and pumps

Well systems also involve a pressure tank, switch and pump that need checking. Short cycling and pressure swings often trace to a waterlogged tank rather than the treatment equipment.

Test before you buy

We test hardness, iron, pH, sulfide and dissolved solids on site. Packaged systems sold without a test are guessing, and on well water guessing is expensive.

Pricing

Water Filtration and Softener Installation Cost

Systems are quoted after the water test, as one installed price including the loop, bypass valves, drain connection and startup. Under-sink RO is the least expensive entry point. A softener plus carbon combination is the most common whole-house setup here.

  • System type. A single cartridge or carbon tank, versus softener plus carbon plus reverse osmosis.
  • Where it goes. A garage with a nearby drain, versus an outdoor or tight install needing new plumbing and drain.
  • Water test result. Moderate hardness and standard chlorine, versus high hardness, iron or sulfur needing specific media.
  • Household demand. A small household on city water, versus a large home or well supply needing higher flow.
  • Bypass and service. A bypass fitted for easy servicing, versus retro-fitting isolation to an awkward existing install.

Before you call

What to Find Out Before Sizing a System

Filtration only works when it is matched to your actual water and your actual household. Start here.

  • Get a water test, or your utility's annual water quality report, for hardness and chlorine.
  • Count bathrooms and occupants; that sets the flow rate and tank size.
  • Decide whether you want whole-home treatment or drinking water only.
  • Find where the main enters the house, and whether there is a drain nearby for a softener.

One thing not to do: Be sceptical of anyone quoting a system before testing your water. Sizing from a sales sheet is how people end up with a softener that regenerates constantly.

Urgency

When Does Filtration Equipment Need Urgent Attention?

Filtration is planned work. Call promptly if:

  • A softener or filter housing is leaking onto the floor or into a wall.
  • The system has stuck in regeneration and is running water to drain continuously.
  • You have lost pressure to the whole house since the unit was fitted.
  • Water has turned discoloured or salty at the taps.

FAQs

Water Filtration FAQs

Do I need a softener if the water is already treated?

Treated means safe, not soft. Boynton Beach water is hard, and softening is what protects your heater and fixtures.

Will softened water taste salty?

No. Ion exchange leaves a small amount of sodium, far less than a slice of bread. Salt-free conditioners are available if sodium is a concern.

Is reverse osmosis worth it?

For drinking and cooking water, yes. It removes dissolved solids that softeners and carbon do not touch.

How much maintenance is involved?

Softeners need salt every few months. Carbon media lasts several years. RO membranes and filters are changed annually.

Can you install a system in a condo?

Often yes, with an under-sink RO or a compact point-of-entry unit. Association rules vary and we check them first.

Do I need a softener, a filter, or both?

A softener addresses hardness, which is what scales your water heater and spots the glassware. Carbon addresses taste, odor and chlorine. Many Palm Beach County homes benefit from both, plus reverse osmosis at the kitchen tap for drinking water, but the water test decides that rather than a package deal.
