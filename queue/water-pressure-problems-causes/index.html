Why Your Water Pressure Dropped

Sudden drops and slow drops have completely different causes.

Low water pressure in a Boynton Beach home usually traces to one of five things. The most useful clue is how it happened. A pressure problem that appeared overnight points somewhere very different from one that crept up over a decade.

Start by working out whether it affects the whole house or one fixture, and whether it is hot, cold or both. Those two answers narrow the list immediately.

Narrow it down first

What the pattern tells you

  • One fixture only. Likely cause: Clogged aerator or a partly closed stop
  • Hot side only. Likely cause: Water heater inlet valve or scale in the heater
  • Whole house, sudden. Likely cause: Pressure regulator, a partly closed main valve or a supply issue
  • Whole house, gradual over years. Likely cause: Narrowed pipe, usually galvanized or scaled
  • Drops when a second tap opens. Likely cause: Undersized supply or a partly blocked main

A gradual, whole house drop in an older home is the classic galvanized signature. Signs galvanized pipe needs replacing covers it.

Check the cheap things first

  1. Unscrew the aerator on the affected faucet and clean it. Hard water blocks these constantly.
  2. Check the angle stop under the sink is fully open.
  3. Check the main shutoff is fully open. A partly closed valve is a surprisingly common cause.
  4. Check the water heater inlet valve if only the hot side is affected.

Hard water is the usual reason aerators block here. Hard water in Palm Beach County explains why they need cleaning more often than in other states.

When it is the regulator

Most homes on city water have a pressure reducing valve where the supply enters. When it fails, pressure either drops across the whole house or climbs to a level that damages fixtures.

It is an inexpensive part and a short job, which makes it worth ruling out before anyone talks about repiping. Signs of a failing regulator covers what to look for.

Frequently asked questions

What is normal house pressure?

Most homes run comfortably in the range a regulator is set to deliver. Too low is a nuisance, and too high quietly damages fixtures and appliances.

Can low pressure damage anything?

Low pressure is mostly an inconvenience. High pressure is the one that causes damage, which is why a failed regulator matters in both directions.

Why is it worse in the morning?

Usually demand on the street rather than a fault in the house. If it is severe and constant, have the house tested.
