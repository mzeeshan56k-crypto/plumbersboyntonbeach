Questions Worth Asking Before You Hire a Plumber

Five questions, asked before the work, prevent most disputes after it.

Hiring a plumber in Boynton Beach goes wrong in predictable ways, and most of them are preventable with a short conversation before anyone is booked. The questions below are not adversarial. A good plumber answers all of them without hesitation.

The aim is simple. Know what you are paying, who is doing the work, and what happens if something is not right afterwards.

The five that matter most

  1. Are you licensed and insured, and may I have the license number?
  2. Is the price flat and written, and does it change if the job runs long?
  3. Is a permit required for this work, and is it included?
  4. What is the warranty on parts and on labor?
  5. What happens if you find something else once the wall is open?

You can verify a Florida license yourself with the Department of Business and Professional Regulation. Asking for the number at the door is normal and nobody should be offended by it.

Questions specific to the job

Ask these depending on the work

  • Repeat drain clog. Question: Will you camera the line, and do I get the footage?
  • Water heater replacement. Question: Is the permit, pan and haul away included?
  • Slab leak. Question: Will you price spot repair, reroute and repipe side by side?
  • Repipe. Question: What material, and how will the drywall be closed?

On a slab leak in particular, a quote offering only the largest option deserves a second opinion. Comparing slab leak options covers what should be priced.

Warning signs

  • A firm price quoted over the phone before anyone has seen the problem.
  • Pressure to decide immediately on a large job.
  • Reluctance to put the price in writing.
  • No mention of a permit on work that clearly needs one.
  • A recommendation to replace without any diagnostic evidence.

How plumbing pricing works covers what a fair quote should contain.

Two topics come up in almost every quote. Permits and, in an older house, what to expect from the plumbing.

Frequently asked questions

How do I verify a Florida plumbing license?

Search the license number on the Florida Department of Business and Professional Regulation website. It takes a minute and it is worth doing.

Should I get three quotes?

For a large job, yes. For an emergency, no. Judgment about urgency is part of the decision.

Is a diagnostic fee normal?

Yes, on jobs where finding the problem is the work. It should be credited against the repair once you approve it.
