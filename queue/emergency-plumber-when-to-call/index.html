When It Is a Real Plumbing Emergency

Paying emergency rates for a dripping tap helps nobody. Here is the line.

Calling an emergency plumber in Boynton Beach makes sense when water is doing damage you cannot stop. It makes less sense for a problem that will sit quietly until morning. Knowing which is which saves money and gets the genuinely urgent calls answered faster.

The test is simple. Is water going somewhere it should not, and can you stop it? If the answer is no, that is an emergency. If you can isolate it at a shutoff and nothing is getting wet, it can usually be scheduled.

Call now

  • Water is flowing and you cannot stop it at any valve.
  • Sewage is coming up through a floor drain, tub or shower.
  • You smell gas anywhere, in which case leave first and call from outside.
  • There is no water at all in the house.
  • Water is reaching electrical outlets, a panel or a unit below yours.

If a line has burst, shutting off the main is the first move and it matters more than the phone call.

It can wait until morning

  • A dripping faucet or a running toilet, as long as nothing is pooling.
  • One slow drain with the rest of the house behaving normally.
  • No hot water, if the tank is not leaking.
  • A water heater that is noisy but dry.

No hot water feels urgent and rarely is. Heater noise and a cold shower are both uncomfortable rather than damaging.

What to do while you wait

  1. Shut the main, or the nearest isolating valve if the problem is one fixture.
  2. Kill power at the breaker to any circuit near standing water.
  3. Move what you can off wet flooring and photograph the damage.
  4. Keep people and pets away from any waste water.
  5. Have the shutoff location and the age of the home ready for the plumber.

Sewage indoors is a health risk as well as a plumbing problem. Stay out of the area and ventilate it if you safely can.
