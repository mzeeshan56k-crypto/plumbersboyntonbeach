Do You Need a Permit to Replace a Water Heater?

Yes. And the inspection is what protects you if it goes wrong later.

A water heater permit in Boynton Beach is required for replacement, not just for new installation. The permit covers the connections, the venting on gas units, the relief valve discharge and the pan and drain where one is needed. An inspector signs it off when the work meets code.

Homeowners sometimes see the permit as a cost to avoid. It is the opposite. It is the part of the job that gives you recourse if the install was wrong, and the part an insurer or a future buyer will ask about.

What the inspection looks at

  • Correct relief valve and a discharge line routed to a safe point.
  • A drain pan and drain line where the unit sits above finished space.
  • Venting sized and sloped correctly on gas units.
  • Seismic or strap requirements where they apply.
  • Correct electrical connection and bonding.

Most failed inspections we see come down to venting or the discharge line, both of which are safety items rather than paperwork.

Who pulls it

A licensed plumber pulls the permit and meets the inspector. That should be included in the quoted price rather than added afterwards, and it is a fair question to ask before you agree to the work.

Permitting rules vary by municipality across the county. Some cities run their own, others route through Palm Beach County, and that affects timing more than cost.

Why it matters later

Unpermitted work surfaces at the worst moment. It comes up during a sale, during an insurance claim after water damage, or when the next plumber opens a wall and finds the install was never signed off.

If your heater is being replaced because the tank failed, what a bottom leak means covers the decision you are making. If it is still sound, maintenance may buy you years.

Permits are part of a replacement, not an extra. Settle sizing and the tank or tankless question before the permit is pulled.

Why the permit exists

A water heater combines pressurized water, a significant energy source and, on a gas unit, combustion products that have to leave the building. Each of those has a failure mode that affects the people in the house rather than just the appliance. The inspection exists because those failure modes are not visible once the installation is finished.

The items an inspector looks at are the ones most commonly skipped by an unpermitted install. Correct relief valve and discharge routing. Seismic or structural strapping where required. A pan with an actual discharge under a unit above finished space. Proper venting with correct materials and clearances on a gas unit. Correct gas line sizing. Bonding and disconnect arrangements on an electric unit.

None of those affect whether you get hot water tomorrow. All of them affect what happens when something goes wrong, which is precisely why they are the first things cut when a job is priced to be cheapest.

What an unpermitted replacement costs you later

  • Insurance friction after a loss. A claim following water damage or a combustion incident can be complicated by work that was never inspected.
  • Problems at sale. Permit history is a matter of public record, and buyers and their agents increasingly check it. An open or missing permit becomes a negotiating point or a condition of closing.
  • The cost of retroactive permitting, which usually exceeds the cost of doing it correctly the first time and may require opening finished work to inspect.
  • No recourse on workmanship. A contractor who did not pull a permit is not the contractor who will come back and stand behind the work.
  • Safety items that were genuinely skipped rather than merely undocumented. In our experience the two go together more often than not.
  • Difficulty with warranty claims, where some manufacturers ask for evidence of professional installation.

The amount saved by skipping the permit is small relative to any one of those outcomes. It is a classic case of a visible saving today against an invisible risk later.

How the process actually runs

  1. The licensed contractor applies for the permit, usually before the work is scheduled. In most cases this is electronic and quick.
  2. The permit is issued and the work proceeds. Nothing about this stage delays a same-day replacement in practice, because reputable contractors handle it as routine paperwork.
  3. The installation is completed with the inspection requirements already satisfied, since the installer knows what will be checked.
  4. An inspection is scheduled. This happens after you have hot water, not before, so it does not leave the household without service.
  5. The inspector attends, checks the work, and either passes it or lists corrections.
  6. The permit is closed out. That closure is the part that matters for your records, and it is the part most commonly left hanging.

Ask specifically whether the permit has been closed, not just pulled. An open permit on a property record causes the same problems at sale as no permit at all.

Questions to ask before you hire

What to confirm in writing

  • Is the permit included in the price. Why it matters: Avoids it appearing as an extra later.
  • Whose license is the permit under. Why it matters: It should be the company doing the work.
  • Who schedules the inspection. Why it matters: Usually the contractor, confirm it.
  • Does the quote include a pan and discharge. Why it matters: Frequently omitted on attic and closet units.
  • Is an expansion tank included if needed. Why it matters: Depends on your line pressure.
  • Is removal of the old unit included. Why it matters: Commonly assumed and sometimes charged separately.

A contractor who treats these as reasonable questions is the one you want. The answers also make quotes comparable, which is otherwise difficult when one price includes the permit, the pan and the expansion tank and another does not.

Frequently asked questions

Can I replace my own water heater?

A homeowner can pull a permit for their own residence in many Florida jurisdictions. The work still has to pass inspection, and gas and venting are where self installs usually fail.

How long does the permit add?

Usually not much. Most replacements are scheduled and inspected within a few days, and the unit is in service the same day it is fitted.

What if my heater was replaced without a permit?

It can often be resolved after the fact. Talk to a licensed plumber before a sale rather than during one.

Do you handle the permit?

Yes, it is part of the price. Call our Boynton Beach plumbers on (833) 567-2788 and we pull it and close it out.

Does a permit mean an inspector comes inside my house?

Yes, briefly, to look at the installation. It is a short visit focused on the heater and its connections rather than a general review of the property. Inspectors are looking at the specific items listed above, and on a correctly installed unit the visit is uneventful.

What if I find out previous work was unpermitted?

It can usually be resolved retroactively, and the earlier it is addressed the simpler it is. A licensed contractor can apply for a permit on existing work and arrange an inspection. Expect the possibility of corrections if something was genuinely done wrong, which is better found now than during a sale.

Is a like-for-like swap really a permitted job?

In most jurisdictions here, yes, because the items being inspected are exactly the ones a swap tends to leave as they were, correct or not. The fact that the old unit was in that position does not establish that the arrangement meets current requirements. Confirm with your building department rather than assuming either way.
