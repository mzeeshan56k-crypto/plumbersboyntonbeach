Leaving a Florida Home Empty: Plumbing Checklist

The leaks that cost the most are the ones nobody was there to notice.

Vacation home plumbing in Boynton Beach has one defining risk. A small leak in an occupied house gets noticed in hours. The same leak in a seasonal home runs for weeks, and the damage is done long before anyone opens the door.

The checklist below takes under an hour and removes most of that risk. The single most important item is the first one.

Before you leave

  1. Shut off the water at the main. This is the one item that matters most.
  2. If you cannot shut the main, close the stops at every toilet and sink.
  3. Turn the water heater to its vacation setting, or shut it off entirely.
  4. Run the dishwasher and washing machine empty, then leave their doors ajar.
  5. Pour a little water into rarely used drains so the traps do not dry out.
  6. Check under every sink for damp before you go.

Dry traps are why an empty house smells of sewer gas when you return. A cup of water in each one prevents it.

If the water must stay on

  • Fit a leak detection shutoff that closes automatically on abnormal flow.
  • Ask someone to check the property regularly and know where the main valve is.
  • Leave the air conditioning running at a sensible setting to control humidity.
  • Replace any braided supply line that is more than a few years old before you leave.

Irrigation is often the reason owners leave the water on. Isolating irrigation from the house supply lets you shut the house off while the yard keeps running.

When you come back

  1. Open the main slowly and listen for running water.
  2. Walk the house and check under every sink and around the heater.
  3. Run each fixture for a minute to refill traps and clear stagnant water.
  4. Check the meter with everything closed before you settle in.

That last check is the two hour meter test in miniature. The full version is worth running on your first day back.

Seasonal owners deal with two calendars. This checklist for leaving, and storm season preparation for while you are away. Both start with knowing your main shutoff.

Why an empty house is the highest risk

A plumbing failure in an occupied house is noticed in minutes. The same failure in a house nobody visits for six weeks runs for six weeks. That single difference is why the worst water damage losses are almost always in properties that were empty, and why the precautions for a seasonal home are different in kind rather than degree.

This climate adds a second factor. Warmth and humidity mean that water left in a closed house produces mold quickly, and the damage from a leak that ran for a month is not just the water but everything the humidity did while nobody opened a window.

The third factor is that the most common failures are connectors rather than pipes. A supply line, a washing machine hose or a toilet fill valve, all of which fail without warning and all of which are permanently pressurized unless the water is off.

The full shutdown procedure

  1. Close the main shutoff valve at the house.
  2. Open the lowest faucets, inside and outside, to drain the pressure and most of the standing water from the lines.
  3. Flush each toilet to empty the tanks, and leave the tank lids on.
  4. Switch the water heater off, or to its vacation setting, so it cannot cycle or run dry.
  5. Close the angle stops at every toilet and under every sink as a second layer, in case the main valve seeps.
  6. Turn off the irrigation at the controller and at its isolation valve, unless a timer is intentionally maintaining the landscaping.
  7. Pour a little water into any floor drain or seldom-used trap, since a trap that dries out lets sewer gas into the house.
  8. Set the air conditioning to a humidity-managing setting rather than switching it off entirely.
  9. Clear the condensate drain line, because a blockage there floods while you are away even with the water off.
  10. Empty and clean the refrigerator ice maker, and shut its supply.

The condensate drain is the one most people miss, and it is the single most common source of water damage in an empty Florida house with the water correctly shut off. The air conditioning runs for months and produces a great deal of condensate, and a blocked line puts all of it somewhere unintended.

When the water has to stay on

Reducing risk without shutting down

  • Automatic leak detection shutoff valve. What it addresses: Closes the main when flow looks abnormal.
  • Point-of-use shutoffs closed. What it addresses: Removes the most likely failure points.
  • Braided stainless connectors throughout. What it addresses: The most common burst component.
  • Water heater on vacation setting. What it addresses: Reduces cycling and standby risk.
  • Pressure regulator verified. What it addresses: Overpressure is behind most connector failures.
  • Pan and discharge under the heater. What it addresses: Contains a tank failure.
  • Someone checking weekly. What it addresses: Shortens how long any failure runs.
  • Water alarms at the heater and laundry. What it addresses: Early warning where there is someone to receive it.

An automatic shutoff valve is the measure that genuinely changes the outcome, because it acts whether anyone is watching or not. For a property that sits empty for months at a time and cannot have the water off, it is the one item worth the cost.

Coming back

  1. Before opening the main, walk through and look at ceilings, under sinks, around the heater and at every floor drain.
  2. Open the main valve slowly. A fast opening sends a pressure surge through a system full of air.
  3. Open one faucet at a time, starting with the lowest and nearest, and let the air out before moving on.
  4. Leave aerators off for the first run so grit flushes out rather than lodging in them.
  5. Run every fixture, hot and cold, and check under each one with a dry hand afterwards.
  6. Turn the water heater back on only once the tank is confirmed full, which matters most on an electric unit.
  7. Flush all the toilets and check for leaks at the base and the supply.
  8. Run the dishwasher and washing machine on an empty cycle and watch the connections.
  9. Read the meter, then read it again after thirty minutes with everything off, to confirm nothing is leaking.
  10. Run water into every drain and check that each one flows, since traps and lines can dry and settle.

Flushing the system properly matters after a long shutdown. Water that has sat still in pipes for months should be run out rather than consumed, and any point-of-use filter cartridge is worth changing at the same time.

Frequently asked questions

Should I shut the water off every time I leave?

If the house will be empty more than a few days, yes. It is the cheapest insurance available against an unattended leak.

Will shutting the water off hurt the plumbing?

No. Valves and pipe are fine without pressure. Dry traps are the only side effect and a cup of water fixes those.

What about the water heater?

Vacation mode or off. There is no reason to keep eighty gallons hot for an empty house.

Should I shut the water off every time I leave?

For anything beyond a few days, yes. It takes a couple of minutes, it costs nothing, and it removes the single largest category of loss a property faces. For a weekend away it matters less, though closing the washing machine and dishwasher valves is still a sensible habit.

Will shutting the water off hurt the plumbing?

No. Pipes are fine empty or full, and the fixtures are unaffected. The two things to watch are traps drying out, which lets odors in and is solved with a little water in each drain, and running the water heater dry, which is solved by switching it off. Neither is a reason to leave the system pressurized.

Can someone check the property for me?

Many plumbing companies will do a scheduled check on a seasonal property, and some property management services include it. The value is in shortening how long a failure runs rather than preventing it, which is why a weekly or fortnightly check is worth considerably more than a monthly one. Combine it with an automatic shutoff valve and the exposure becomes small.
