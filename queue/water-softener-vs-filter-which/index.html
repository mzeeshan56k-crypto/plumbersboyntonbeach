Water Softener or Filter: Which Do You Need?

One treats hardness. The other treats taste. They are not interchangeable.

Choosing between a water softener and a filter in Boynton Beach starts with knowing what you are trying to fix. A softener removes the hardness minerals that scale your water heater and spot your glassware. A carbon filter addresses taste, odor and chlorine. They solve different problems.

Many homes here benefit from both, plus reverse osmosis at the kitchen tap for drinking water. What decides it is a water test, not a package deal.

What each one does

Softener compared with filtration

  • Softener. Treats: Calcium and magnesium hardness Noticeable result: Less scale, better lather, longer heater life
  • Carbon filter. Treats: Chlorine, taste and odor Noticeable result: Water tastes and smells better
  • Sediment filter. Treats: Grit and particles Noticeable result: Protects valves and cartridges
  • Reverse osmosis. Treats: Dissolved solids at one tap Noticeable result: Drinking water quality

Hardness is the one that costs money rather than comfort. What hard water does to plumbing covers the running cost of leaving it untreated.

Test before you buy

A water test gives hardness in grains per gallon and shows what else is present. Sizing follows from that result and from your household demand, not from a shelf package.

Be wary of a quote given before anyone has tested your water. That is how people end up with a softener that regenerates constantly or a system sized for a different house.

Where it goes

  • Near where the main enters the house, so the whole home is covered.
  • Within reach of a drain, because a softener discharges during regeneration.
  • Somewhere with service access for media and filter changes.
  • With a bypass fitted, so the house keeps water while the unit is serviced.

If you have a tankless heater, treating the supply changes your service interval considerably. Descaling schedules cover that.

Treatment pays for itself through the equipment it protects. Water heater lifespan is the clearest example, and reverse osmosis handles the drinking tap separately.

Frequently asked questions

Does a softener make water taste salty?

No, when it is set up correctly. Sodium levels rise slightly, which is why many homes add reverse osmosis at the drinking tap.

Do I need both a softener and a filter?

Many homes here do, because hardness and chlorine are separate issues. The test tells you whether both are justified.

How often does a softener need salt?

It varies with hardness and household use. A correctly sized unit usually needs topping up every few weeks rather than weekly.
