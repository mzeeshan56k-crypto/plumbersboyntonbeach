Reverse Osmosis for Drinking Water at Home

It treats one tap very well rather than the whole house adequately.

Reverse osmosis in a Boynton Beach home is a point of use system, usually fitted under the kitchen sink. It pushes water through a membrane that holds back dissolved solids, which produces drinking water of a quality whole house filtration does not attempt.

That focus is the point. Treating every gallon in the house to drinking standard would be slow and wasteful. Treating the one tap you actually drink from is efficient.

How the system is built

  1. A sediment pre-filter protects the membrane from grit.
  2. A carbon pre-filter removes chlorine, which damages the membrane.
  3. The membrane itself separates dissolved solids.
  4. A storage tank holds treated water so the tap delivers instantly.
  5. A post filter polishes taste on the way out.

Pre-filters are what keep the membrane alive. Changing them on schedule costs little and protects the most expensive part of the system.

What it needs from your plumbing

  • A cold supply connection under the sink.
  • A drain connection for the reject water.
  • Space for the tank, usually in the sink base.
  • A dedicated tap on the sink deck or a hole in the counter.
  • Adequate incoming pressure for the membrane to work properly.

Pressure matters more than people expect. If the house pressure is low, output drops. Causes of low water pressure covers how to check.

Softener first or RO first

If the water is hard, softening upstream extends membrane life considerably. Hardness scales the membrane the same way it scales a heater.

That is why the usual order is softener for the house and reverse osmosis at the kitchen tap. Choosing between a softener and a filter covers the decision.

Reverse osmosis works best as part of a plan rather than on its own. Hard water is what shortens membrane life, and on a private well well treatment comes first.

Frequently asked questions

Does reverse osmosis waste water?

Some water goes to drain with the rejected solids. Modern systems are far more efficient than older ones, and the volume is small against household use.

How often do filters need changing?

Pre-filters commonly every six to twelve months and the membrane every few years. Hard water shortens both.

Can it feed the fridge ice maker?

Yes, and it is a common addition. It needs a line run from the system to the fridge.
