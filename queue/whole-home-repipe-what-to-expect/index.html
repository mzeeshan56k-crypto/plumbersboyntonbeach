What a Whole Home Repipe Actually Involves

Most homes stay occupied. The disruption is drywall, not plumbing.

A whole home repipe in Boynton Beach replaces the supply lines that feed your fixtures. It does not touch the drains. That distinction surprises many owners, and it is the reason a repipe is usually less disruptive than the word suggests.

New lines are run through the attic and dropped inside walls to each fixture. The old pipe is generally left in place. Water is restored at the end of each working day, so most households stay in the house throughout.

The sequence

  1. Walkthrough and plan, including where openings will be made.
  2. New lines run through the attic and down to each fixture.
  3. Tie in at each fixture and at the water heater.
  4. Pressure test the whole system and have it inspected.
  5. Close and patch the drywall openings.
  6. Final check of pressure and flow at every fixture.

The pressure test and inspection come before anything is closed up. That order is not negotiable and it is what the permit exists to confirm.

What the disruption actually is

Expect small openings in drywall at each drop, usually near fixtures and in a few hallway or closet walls. A competent crew makes the smallest openings that allow the work and protects floors and furniture as they go.

Expect to be without water for parts of the working day. Expect dust. Expect it to look worse on day two than it does at the end.

How long and what it depends on

What drives the timeline

  • Number of bathrooms. Effect: More fixtures means more drops and more tie ins
  • Single or two story. Effect: A second story adds routing complexity
  • Attic access. Effect: Good access speeds everything up
  • Finishes. Effect: Tile and stone walls slow the work and the restoration

Material choice has less impact on the schedule than most people assume. Comparing PEX, CPVC and copper covers that decision separately.

Most repipes here start for one of two reasons. Polybutylene that insurers will not accept, or galvanized pipe that has closed up.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to move out?

Usually not. Water goes back on at the end of each day, and the work is staged so the house stays liveable.

Is the old pipe removed?

Generally no. Pulling it out means opening far more wall than leaving it in place, and it serves no purpose once it is isolated.

Does a repipe fix low pressure?

Often yes, if the cause was narrowed pipe. If the cause is a regulator, that is separate. Causes of low water pressure covers both.
