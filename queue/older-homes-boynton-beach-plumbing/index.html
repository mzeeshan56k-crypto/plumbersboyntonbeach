Plumbing in an Older Boynton Beach Home

Three materials and one slab explain most of the work in these houses.

Older home plumbing in Boynton Beach follows a fairly predictable pattern. Houses built before 1980 here were generally built on slab, with copper supply lines routed through the concrete and cast iron drains buried underneath. Both materials were good choices at the time and both are now at or past their service life.

If you own one of these homes, or are about to buy one, knowing the pattern is worth more than any single inspection. It tells you what to look for and roughly when.

The four things that define these houses

What older Boynton Beach homes have in common

  • Slab construction. What it means: Supply leaks happen under concrete and are hard to see
  • Slab routed copper. What it means: Pinhole leaks appear with no warning
  • Cast iron drains. What it means: Scale narrows the line and clogs become routine
  • Shallow water table. What it means: Anything buried sits in damp ground year round

Those four together explain most of the plumbing work in this city. Slab leak signs and cast iron drain failure cover the two biggest.

What to check first

  1. Walk the floor barefoot and note any warm patch.
  2. Run the meter test to confirm there is no hidden supply leak.
  3. Look under sinks and at the water heater to identify the pipe material.
  4. Ask whether the main line has ever backed up, and how often.
  5. Check the water heater age and whether the anode has ever been changed.

If the house has had more than one pinhole leak, the count matters more than the individual repairs. Why pinholes repeat explains the pattern.

Buying one

A camera inspection of the sewer lateral is the single best money spent before closing on a pre 1980 home here. It is inexpensive and it finds the one problem that is genuinely expensive to inherit.

Ask for permit history on any water heater, repipe or sewer work. What needs a permit covers which jobs should have one on file.

Frequently asked questions

Should I repipe preemptively?

Not on age alone. Repipe when the leak count says the system is finished, not because the house reached a birthday.

Is cast iron always a problem?

Not always, but it is always worth inspecting in a house this age. Some lines are scaled and serviceable, others are cracked.

What is the first thing to budget for?

Usually the water heater, because it has the shortest life of anything in the system here.
