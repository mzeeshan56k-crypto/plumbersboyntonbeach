How Plumbers Find a Leak You Cannot See

Opening the wall is the last step, not the first.

Finding hidden leaks in a Boynton Beach home is a process of narrowing, not guessing. The goal is to put the leak within inches before anything is cut, because demolition is the most expensive way to search for water.

Three tools do most of the work. Pressure isolation tells us which part of the system is losing water. Acoustic equipment listens for the sound of water escaping under pressure. Thermal imaging shows where a hot line is warming the surface above it.

Step one: confirm there is a leak

Before any equipment comes out, the meter answers the basic question. If the meter moves with everything closed, water is leaving the system.

You can run that yourself in two hours. The water meter test is free and it saves diagnostic time on the visit.

Step two: isolate the zone

Shutting valves one at a time divides the system into sections. Isolating the irrigation is usually first, because yard leaks are common and the repair is completely different.

Isolating the water heater separates hot from cold. If the sound stops when the heater inlet closes, the leak is on the hot side, which often means a slab line.

Step three: pinpoint it

What each tool does

  • Acoustic listening. What it finds: The sound of pressurised water escaping Limits: Harder in noisy or carpeted areas
  • Thermal imaging. What it finds: Heat from a hot line warming a surface Limits: Works best on hot lines, not cold
  • Pressure isolation. What it finds: Which branch is losing water Limits: Narrows the area, not the exact point
  • Tracer gas. What it finds: Very small leaks acoustic cannot hear Limits: Specialist, used when others fall short

Together they usually place a slab leak within inches. What happens after a slab leak is located covers the repair choice.

Frequently asked questions

Is leak detection worth paying for?

Yes, because the alternative is opening floors and walls to look. Locating costs a fraction of unnecessary demolition.

How accurate is it?

On a pressurised line under a slab, usually within inches. Accuracy drops on very small leaks and in noisy environments, which is where tracer gas helps.

Can you find a drain leak the same way?

No. Drains are not pressurised, so they are inspected with a camera instead. Camera inspection covers that.
